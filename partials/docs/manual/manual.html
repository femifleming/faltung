Partials
An additive synthesizer you can see. Manual.

Partials builds every note out of up to 516 sine waves, called partials. Almost everything in the top half of the window changes those partials before they are turned into sound: how loud each one is, where it sits in pitch, and how that changes over time. The display on the right shows them as you play.

How to read the pictures in this manual. Every dark picture was made by the synth itself. Time runs left to right, pitch runs bottom to top, and each horizontal line is one partial. In most of them one note is held while one control is moved from one end to the other, so you can see what that control does. Brightness is tilted a little towards the top so high partials stay visible.

Where everything is

The window is laid out like Harmor's, section for section: if you know that synth, every control is where your hand expects it. The look is Partials' own. Where Harmor has a knob there is a bar with its value above and its name below; where it has a fader there is a standing bar; where it has a switch with two or three positions the positions are written out and the one in use is filled.

Getting around

A barDrag sideways or up and down. Hold Shift for fine steps. Double-click to reset. The mouse wheel also works. The value is the small number above it.
A standing barThe same, upright. Its value shows while the mouse is on it.
A number in a slanted boxDrag up or down.
A value with ▾A menu. Click it.
slanted boxA switch: filled = on. Click it. Where the choices are written out (oct / hz, lin / log), click the one you want.
Right-click a barReset, copy the value to the other part, open its articulator (envelope, LFO, mappings) in the editor, or quick-map it to X, Y or Z.
Orange mark on a barSomething is moving this control: an envelope, an LFO or a mapping is switched on for it.
Partial displayShows the newest note of the part you are looking at. Hover to read a frequency.
A and BTwo complete synths. The big A / B buttons choose which one the controls show. The slanted boxes at the ends of the mix bar switch them on. "link" makes every edit apply to both.
SoundsBuilt-in sounds, your saved ones (in Music / Partials / Presets), save, open, copy / paste of a whole sound or one part, chorus and reverb presets: the arrow next to "global", the list button on the left edge, or the name in the info box.
Window sizeDrag the bottom-right corner; everything scales. The cube button on the left edge has fixed sizes.

Timbre

The two small pictures at the top left are timbre 1 and timbre 2: the starting waveforms. Timbre 1 starts as a saw (every partial, each one quieter than the last), timbre 2 as a square (every other partial). Click a picture to draw its partials in the editor. Right-click it for saw, square, triangle, sine, random, or to analyze a single-cycle wave file. You can also drop a wave file on it.

Blend menu (next to "timbre")How the two timbres combine: Fade, Subtract, Multiply, Maximum, Minimum, or Pluck (timbre 2 appears as the pluck dies away).
startWhere in the wave each note starts, 0 to 360°.
randLeft: each partial gets its own random phase. Right: the whole wave starts at a random point on each note.
The small button with three barsSub configuration: around the fundamental, or below it.
The button under clipThe clipping mode. The drawing on it is the curve in use.
env / volVolume before the partials become sound (env) and after (vol).
autoAuto-gain. Evens out the level when shaping removes or adds a lot. Its mode (Absolute aims at a fixed level, Relative at the level before shaping) is on the ADV tab.
fxHow much of this part skips the effects. 0% is all through the effects, 100% all dry. Negative values flip the dry signal.
panWhere the part sits between left and right.
vel: time, scale, relWhat key velocity does to the volume envelope: skip part of the attack, change the attack speed, change the release length. Velocity to volume is the "vel" switch in Global.

Pitch, glide, unison

freq, detuneTwo numbers each: multiply / divide. freq 1 / 2 is an octave down, 2 / 1 an octave up. detune spreads the partials (see the pictures).
pitchThis part's pitch, two octaves either way. "env" scales its pitch envelope. The pitch bar in Global moves both parts.
LEGATO timeLeft of centre: milliseconds (up to 1.5 s). Right: steps of the host tempo. "limit": portamento never slides further than this. Legato and portamento themselves are switched on in Global.
lin / log, fix / varStraight or curved slide; the same time for every slide, or longer for bigger jumps.
UNISON order1 to 9 copies. "pan" spreads them left and right, "phase" at 100% (blur) gives each copy random phases, lower values space them evenly, "alt" flips every second copy in the right channel for extra width.
Unison typeClassic (wide), Uniform (even), Blurred (drifting), Random, Hz (same spread in Hz for every partial).
STRUM tensionLeft: the notes speed up through the chord. Right: they slow down. The menu sets the direction: Up, Down, Alt, Random.

Filters

Two filters. They work on the partials, so they add nothing of their own: a partial is simply made louder or quieter. Each picture below holds one note while freq is swept from high to low.

Resonance types

Same sweep each time, res at 80%.

envHow far the filter-frequency envelope reaches. Nothing happens until that envelope is switched on (right-click freq, Edit articulator, then the switch under the graph). The envelope works below the freq bar: at the top of the graph the filter sits where freq is, at the bottom it is lower by the env amount. So with freq fully open and env at 100% (how a new sound starts) the envelope is heard at once. A negative amount turns the graph upside down.
width (right half)Width of the resonance bump. "adapt" under it keeps it sounding the same up and down the keyboard.
oct / hzWhether the filter (left) and its resonance (right) are measured in octaves or in Hz.
adapt (left)Shortens the filter envelope for high notes.
1 > 2 mixRight (ser): filter 1 feeds filter 2. Centre: filter 1 only. Left (par): both side by side, ending with filter 2 only.
eq mixHow much of the Global EQ graph is applied. The "curves" button opens the global EQ (fixed frequencies) or the local EQ (follows the note) in the editor.
Custom shape 1 / 2Draw your own filter or resonance curve in the editor (target menu, Shaping).

Blur and pluck

top, bottomTop tension limits how far blur spreads upwards. Bottom sets how loud the blurred partials are.
blur decayGives the pluck a slower, softer tail.
Pluck shapeIn the editor (Shaping): which partials die fastest.

Phaser

A moving pattern of notches. Each picture is one held note with the phaser sweeping.

speedCentre is still. Right sweeps up, left sweeps down; further out is faster. Shown as the number of steps one sweep takes.
kbShifts the pattern with the note.
scaleNotches spaced in octaves, in Hz, or by harmonic number.
lfoLets a slow LFO move the width.
freq / customFreq bends the pitch of the partials with the Phaser shape graph; custom uses that graph as the notch shape.

Prism and harmonizer

from volPrism moves loud partials more (on) or quiet ones more (inv).
amt, width, strHow much harmonizer, how high up it reaches, how strong the copies are.
shift, gap: ofsMoves the shift or gap pattern up in octaves.
shift, gap: stepOne box for the step and how it counts: drag up for adding steps (+1 to +8), down for multiplying ones (x1 to x8).

Global, and the X / Y / Z pad

X, Y and Z are three hand controls that do nothing until you point them at something. Then one move of the pad can change as many things as you like, each in its own way.

Move itDrag in the pad for X and Y. Z: the mouse wheel over the pad, or drag up and down with the middle button (or with Alt held). The three small bars x, y, z do the same one at a time.
Point it at something, the quick wayRight-click any bar that can be articulated and choose Quick map to X (or Y, Z). The pad then sweeps that control over its travel, and the mapping opens in the editor so you can redraw it.
Point it at something, by handIn the editor choose the target, then the part "Modulation X mapping" (or Y, Z), and draw. Editing the line switches the mapping on. Every target has its own three mappings, so X can open a filter, slow an LFO and pan the sound at once.
Automate itModulation X, Y and Z are host parameters: draw them in your DAW or record the pad.
MIDIX follows controller 1 (the mod wheel), Y controller 74, Z controller 71. Right-click the pad or one of x, y, z for MIDI learn (then move the controller you want), to forget a controller, or to go back to these three. The choice is saved with the sound.
SmoothingPad moves are smoothed (about 30 ms) so they never click. For hard, percussive jumps switch "smooth mod" off (right-click the pad, or the ADV tab).
Mod X speedOne more mapping: how fast X is moving. A quick stroke across the pad can open something that a slow move leaves alone.
volMaster volume, with the output meter under it.
lfoScales every LFO of the part on show.
legato, portaLegato: overlapping notes bend one voice instead of starting new ones. Porta: every new note slides in from the last. Their time and curve are in the part's LEGATO section.
limitLegato only happens within this distance; a bigger jump starts a normal new note. Off: always legato.
vel (next to legato)Harder notes slide faster.
vel (under lfo)Velocity changes the volume.
pre, postVolume into and out of the effects.
pitchPitch of the whole instrument, two octaves either way.
strumA chord pressed at once is spread out: direction (Up, Down, Alt, Random), time, and tension (left: speeding up, right: slowing down).

The editor: envelopes, LFOs, mappings

This is where things move. Choose what to move in the first menu (the target), and with what in the second (the part): an envelope, an LFO, or one of nine mappings. Each target can have all of them switched on at once. The slanted box at the bottom left of the graph switches the part on show on and off; touching the graph switches it on for you.

The target menu

ArticulationThings that can move during a note: volume, panning, filter frequency, pitch, phaser, prism and so on. Each has an envelope, an LFO and nine mappings.
MappingThings set per note: envelope attack / decay / release scales, LFO speed and amount, unison phase, predelay. Mappings only.
ShapingOne graph each: the partials of timbre 1 and 2, their phases, EQs, custom filter and resonance shapes, pluck shape, masks that keep an effect away from part of the range.
◂ onIn both menus, next to everything that is switched on.

An envelope

Add a pointRight-click or double-click the background.
Move a pointDrag it. Shift keeps its height, Ctrl or Cmd keeps its time.
Delete a pointAlt-click it, or right-click it and choose Delete.
Bend a lineDrag the small circle in the middle of a segment. Ctrl- or Cmd-drag for fine steps. Right-click the circle to straighten.
Right-click a pointSegment type (hold, smooth, curves, stairs, pulse, wave...), decay start (D), sustain / loop end (S), loop start (L), arpeggiator break.
D, S, LUp to D is the attack, D to S the decay, after S the release. While the key is held the envelope waits at S, or goes round between L and S if there is an L.
att, dec, sus, relFour modifiers under the graph. They leave your drawing alone and stretch or shrink its attack, decay and release (x0.06 to x16) and scale the level of the S point (x0 to x2). An orange line shows the envelope as it now plays, and the info box gives the resulting time. They are host parameters, one set for every envelope, so they can be automated.
tempo / globalTempo: times are in beats. Global: all notes share one envelope, restarted by each new note.
smoothing (the bar next to the part menu)Rounds off sudden jumps of the target.
lock / step / snap / slideLock against changes; draw freehand, one point per grid step (Shift for flat steps); snap to the grid; moving a point moves everything after it.
ZoomThe mouse wheel over the graph zooms the time axis; drag the strip under it to scroll, double-click the strip to see everything.
optionsReset, flip, normalize, decimate, smooth up, scale levels, copy to the other part, copy / paste, make all loop, follow an audio file, create a step sequence. For shapes: draw per harmonic (bars).
The white lineWhere the newest note is in the envelope.
Volume envelopeOn from the start in every new sound: 2 ms attack, 300 ms decay, 100 % sustain, 150 ms release. Switched off, a note simply starts and stops with the key.

An LFO

The handleDrag it up and down for the amplitude; below the middle the wave is turned over. Drag it to the right to make the LFO start later. Right-click to add more points: the line then sets the amplitude over time (fade in, fade out).
amt, spd, tns, skew, pw, phaseAmount, speed, and the shape: tension 0 is a triangle, 32% a sine, 100% a square; skew leans it into a saw; pw makes the top wider or narrower than the bottom. Touching any of them switches the LFO on.
tempo / globalSpeed in beats; one LFO shared by all notes.
lfo in GlobalScales all LFOs together. "Global LFO amount / speed / phase" in the Mapping column let a key, a velocity, the pad or the unison voice change every LFO at once.

The mappings

Keyboard, VelocityLeft to right is low to high note, or soft to hard.
Modulation X, Y, ZThe pad. See above.
RandomA new random position along the graph for each note.
Unison indexA value per unison voice: left is the first voice, right the last (the grid shows them). It works for every target: two voices an octave apart, one bright and one dark, each with its own LFO phase...
Held indexFirst held note, second, third...
Mod X speedHow fast X is moving.

How a control and its graphs combine

Volume, mixes, widths, amounts (unipolar product)The control is the maximum. The graphs scale it down: top of the graph is the control's value, bottom is zero. So a control left at 0% gives nothing to scale: raise it first. The info box says so when that is the case.
Prism, fx, filter mix, LFO amount (bipolar product)The same, but the middle of the graph is zero and the bottom is the control's value turned negative.
Pitch, panning, filter frequency, offsets (bipolar sum)The control is the centre. Graphs add to it: the middle line is no change. For pitch the top is +4 octaves and the bottom -4.
Filter frequency envelopeThe exception: the top of the graph is "no change" and the filter's env bar sets how far below the freq bar the bottom reaches (a negative amount: how far the top reaches, upside down). Sounds saved before this version keep the earlier rule, in which the envelope was added above the freq bar.

The IMG page

Drop a sound file or a picture on the window. A sound is taken apart into partials and can then be played at any speed and pitch (resynthesis). A picture is played directly: left to right is time, bottom to top is partial 1 to 516, brightness is level.

Click in the imageSets the start position (time). Shift-drag selects a stretch. Ctrl-drag or Cmd-drag sets the loop region.
speed, coarsePlayback speed, backwards when negative; coarse multiplies it up to x64 or down to almost stopped.
smoothHow gently a moved start position is followed. Right of centre, big jumps are instant.
loopingOne-shot, Loop, Ping pong.
sharpFor sounds: puts the partials back in step at each attack so transients stay crisp.
LEVEL scale, mixLoudness of the picture; at mix 0% the picture is ignored and you hear the plain timbre.
FREQUENCY scale, modeHow strongly, and how, the frequency plane bends the partials: by octaves, Hz, wide Hz, or as unison detune.
curveHow one column blends into the next: Block, Cap, Bell, Linear, Cubic, Rise, Fall.
planeLook at the level picture (Gain), the pitch-bend picture (Freq), or both.
optionsAnalyze audio, open picture, high precision analysis (516 bands, tuned to the sound, instead of 255), convert to image synthesis, save as PNG, loop region, map slices to keys, time envelopes, random cloud, degrade, crossfade, crop, invert, flip.

The FX page

Distortion

A sine wave through each type at amount 60%.

asymUp: lopsided. Down: lopsided in opposite directions left and right.
mix50% is distorted only, 0% an even blend, 100% clean. Negative flips it.
high cutSoftens the result.

Chorus, delay, reverb, compression

Chorusorder = number of voices (0 is off). The presets menu fills in classic settings. mix: centre dry, right chorus, left flipped chorus.
Delay timeRight of centre: steps of the tempo. Left: milliseconds. Modes: Normal, Inverted (left and right swap each echo), Ping pong.
ReverbPresets, color, input low and high cut, predelay, room size, diffusion, decay, damping, wet.
CompressionLimiting holds the peaks. Warming, Heating and Burning squeeze three bands and lift the low end more each time. low / mid / high set the band levels.
pre / postVolume into and out of the effects: the two standing bars in Global.

The ADV page

delayPredelay: waits before the note starts. Left: milliseconds. Right: tempo steps. Its mappings (target menu, Mapping, Predelay) can make it depend on the key, the velocity or the pad.
auto-gainRelative or Absolute, for the "auto" bar of the part.
smooth modSmoothing of the pad (X, Y, Z).
mod controllersThe MIDI controller of X, Y and Z. Click one for MIDI learn.
poly relBelow 100%, a new note shortens the release tails still sounding.
declickA very short fade at the start and end of notes.
UNIT ORDERThe order the shaping stages run in. Click an entry to move it later, right-click to move it earlier. Example: put HARM PROT after HARM CLIP to keep the fundamental safe from clipping.
EFFECTS ORDERThe same for the five effects.
randomnessWhether random values are new per note, per part, per unison copy, or never change.
side (a/b as stereo)Load the same stereo sound into A and B: A carries the middle, B the sides, and the A/B mix sets the width.
partialsFewer partials uses less processor and loses the top end.
precisionHow often the shaping is recalculated: Average about every 5 ms, High 2.7 ms, Perfect 1.3 ms. "HQ when rendering" uses Perfect for offline bounces.
VOICES monoOff (16-voice polyphony, the default); a repeated note cuts itself (Kill same); any new note cuts all (Kill all); or sends them into release (Release all). Glide legato also makes overlapping notes share one voice; leave it off for chords. The voice count is shown at the bottom of the partial display.

Good to know

16 notes at onceEach with up to 9 unison copies of up to 516 partials. Low notes with high unison cost the most.
It can get loudHigh resonance with self-oscillation, or harmonizer and blur at full, add a lot of level. Auto-gain or the Limiting compressor help.
Saved presetsPlain text files ending in .partials. Drop one on the window to load it.
Older sessionsEvery parameter that existed keeps its name, range and place; sounds saved with earlier versions load and sound as they did.
Not in this versionCopying pictures through the clipboard, selecting and copy / paste of single line points, envelope state files, the envelope filter tool, frozen and bipolar-tension LFOs, and the step-sequence editing window (there is a menu of ready-made sequences instead).
