# sndstudio — what the reference artists do, and which feature covers it

Compiled from interviews and articles (sources linked); where an artist does not discuss gear that is said rather
than guessed. The right-hand column names the sndstudio feature that covers the technique.

| artist | documented methods and techniques | sndstudio feature |
|---|---|---|

[RBMA](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2012/11/ricardo-villalobos-interview), [Crack](https://crackmagazine.net/article/long-reads/ricardo-villalobos-invited-us-into-his-berlin-home/) | audio tracks + resample project (capture a jam, cut it on the timeline); polymeter (per-track length, speed), free clock; `st:colour` console colour stage; sub-only bd1 tuning; `capture` (one click: a new audio track records the master while you play, to cut up afterwards); the bar · minute readout in the header and under the song list |

[XLR8R](https://xlr8r.com/features/romania-exquisitely-elusive/), [fabric](https://fabriclondon.com/posts/in-profile-a-potted-history-of-petre-inspirescu) | the drum engines with per-step pitch, decay locks and slides; mapped lfos drifting slowly; `st:throw` and rv5 on sends; audio clips with repitch, stretch and warp for acoustic fragments, `st:sampler` for the chopped ones; the 8-minute "long form" template |

| **Mark Fell** (snd) | TX81Z / DX100 4-op FM presets "tweaked a little"; each step a ratio of a base tempo (a triplet next to a straight one), one slider stretches a whole pattern; two elements at a time; FM moving harmonics instead of filter sweeps; drawn, non-4/4 material. [FACT](https://factmag.com/2018/10/06/mark-fell-signal-path), [Stray Landings](https://straylandings.co.uk/_content/interviews/2016-09-07-mark-fell-interview/) | a time per step (`step time`, x0.25 … x4: a triplet next to a straight step), the tools `ratios` (type the series: 1 1 2/3 2/3 2/3 3/2), `accelerate` (each step a constant factor of the one before, the loop keeps its length), `even`, `strict` (velocities on 1 … 16 levels, one note length); pattern speed x1/4 … x8 and the free clock in ms per step; `st:fm` 4-operator percussion with presets and a morph towards any preset; the Fell look of the suite |
| **Grischa Lichtenberger** (raster) | Reason and Ableton; one program feeding another and back (feedback between computers); a noise suppressor used as a synthesizer; music from sequencing single tones; massive plug-in chains per track; resampling old tracks into collages. [CDM](https://cdm.link/2016/12/raster-notons-elusive-grischa-lichtenberger-on-creative-sound/) | routes between tracks; a route that closes a loop becomes a feedback route (one buffer late, soft-limited so the loop cannot run away); 12 inserts per track; resample track / project; the gate / expander in `st:strip` as a creative processor; one-tone tracks with per-step envelope locks |
| **Second Woman** (Dietrich, Eustis) | Maschine, Max for Live, NI Razor; K-Devices Tragedy note repeater (synced or free); a Max clock where sliders set each step's length — no tempo, no meter; "the tempo knob as an instrument"; dub reduced to nothing; sub-audio kick tuning, soft-clip on the 2-mix. [FACT](https://factmag.com/2017/05/13/second-woman-interview) | free clock per track (ms per step), tempo as an automation lane and a modulator target; ratchets up to 8 with a curve (the hits crowd in or spread out); `st:repeat` with pitch; `st:throw`; `st:clip` on the master; bd1 tune down to 30 hz |
| **Ryoji Ikeda** | pure tones, sine waves, white noise as the only material; data sonified as binary patterns ("very brutal"); 100 sines tuned to historical pitches, two sines beating; custom software per piece, spreadsheets as scores. [FACT](https://factmag.com/2023/04/13/ryoji-ikeda-interview), [RBMA](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2017/10/ikeda-tech-feature) | `st:tone` precision generator (sine / pulse / click / impulse / noise, exact hz and ms, dc-free); `st:data` byte-to-pulse noise bursts; steps down to 1/128 and the ms clock; `st:clip` digital clipping; export without dither at 32-bit float; master width up to 64 channels |
| **Noisia** | Cubase; iZotope Trash on almost every bass, multiband with a clipper, convolution box modelling; FM8 patches cloned between bass and lead; drums in groups, distorted for room; sound-design detours resampled into samples ("the mix is the music"). [iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/noisia), [UKF](https://ukf.com/read/oh-oh-its-a-massive-noisia-interview/) | wb1 reese / fm growl / neuro presets; `st:multiband` distortion; ms0 multiband / ott; resample track to a new sampler track in one click; `st:sidechain` from any track; routes as drum groups with `st:colour` |
| **Current Value** | starts with the bassline, "the bass leads everything"; Nord Modular G2 and VCV Rack; sidechains on the stereo master while sketching; transient design on snares; a track in an afternoon. [UKF](https://ukf.com/read/current-value-the-rising-stock-of-dbs-most-daring-loner), [wearestudio](https://wearestudio.fr/?p=86403) | the `bass first (half time, 172)` template; `st:transient`; `st:sidechain` with the master as key; hosted VST3 / AU (modular synths); 170–175 bpm half-time template grid |
| **Shigeto** | Reason NN-XT, Ableton; own drums recorded with one condenser mic in a big room, heavily compressed, sampled and chopped; Rhodes, Micromoog, Pocket Piano; palettes of field recordings turned into several sketches; no brickwall. [XLR8R](https://xlr8r.com/gear/in-the-studio-shigeto/), [Tape Op](https://tapeop.com/interviews/168/shigeto-is-zach-saginaw) | audio recording onto tracks; `st:sampler` slicing on onsets to steps (pitch and decay per step as parameter locks); gl8 room compression; stems export; ms0 only when wanted |
| **Four Tet** | edits audio directly in Live's arrangement, off the grid, slip and nudge by ear; a sample folder since the '90s, auditions hundreds of files; presets, "no sound design"; mixes and masters himself; MIDI lines reversed and shortened, swing on MIDI. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4926607), [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/four-tet-tape-notes) | audio clips with grid-free moves (snap off), split / trim / nudge (`,` and `.` nudge the clip you last touched by 5 ms); the browser auditions files on a click; pattern tools (reverse, rotate, shorten); swing per track; ms0 on the master with the auto starting point |
| **Prefuse 73** | two turntables into an MPC; instant mute-group switching of whole sample groups as the glitch; MC vocals chopped into another layer of music; careless chops, no bpm matching. [XLR8R](https://xlr8r.com/?p=115725) | choke groups across tracks (a hit on one track of a group cuts the others), mutes as per-step locks or from a held key, `st:sampler` slices; vocal cut-up with per-step pitch and slices; audio input (turntable / line) recording; `st:repeat` stutters |

[FACT](https://factmag.com/2013/09/11/dont-sweat-the-technique-lapalux-on-brainfeeder-his-debut-album-and-cassette-tape-nostalgia), [Splice](https://splice.com/blog/lapalux-q-and-a/) | `st:vinyl` (wow, flutter, crackle, bandwidth, saturation); `st:throw` tape echo; drift modulators on any pitch; resampling; clip-level stutters with split / duplicate; the external return path via routes |

[5mag](https://5mag.net/?p=14626), [909originals](https://909originals.com/2021/03/22/interview-todd-edwards-discusses-new-single-the-chant-whats-next-for-daft-punk-and-the-inspiration-behind-his-idiosyncratic-production-style/) | `st:sampler` syllable slicing with per-step pitch and chords stacked on a step; wb1 square sub; mpc-style swing templates (sw6 shapes as modulators and per-track swing); the `two-step (garage, 132)` template |

## the others named across the projects

Artists named for the other instruments of the suite and its neighbours; their methods are mapped onto the studio as
well. Same columns, same rule: what a source does not say is marked as missing, not filled in.

| artist | documented methods and techniques | sndstudio feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Oval** (Markus Popp) | early Oval (Popp, Oschatz, Metzger): music from "deliberately mutilated discs", rental-shop CDs marked with non-permanent marker until the player skips, the skips sampled; *Systemisch* as loop compositions on an entry-level Yamaha sampler with 50 seconds of memory, the "weird little loops" kept on floppy disks; later on a PowerBook: tens of thousands of tiny sound files, grouped and regrouped with Mac OS colour labels, a standard sequencer used at "five percent of its potential" only to place them in time, no synthesizers; ovalprocess (programmed by Richard Ross) as a public model of that workflow, "a kind of Tetris for sound", "enabling the user / audience to configure, layout and perform customized recordings oval-style"; *o* (2010) turns to notes, scales and playing. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/oval-markus-popp), [Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/lists/oval-album-guide), [FACT](https://factmag.com/2013/04/09/it-was-sort-of-commercial-suicide-oval-revisits-his-ambient-masterpiece-94diskont), [Ars Electronica](https://webarchive.ars.electronica.art/festival2001/texte/ovalprocess_e.html) | `st:skip` — a sample read like a skipping disc: a fragment repeats, the read point jumps on or back or somewhere else, read errors drop out or hold a frame; `intensity` sets how broken it is, fragments on the grid or in ms, two pointers bound the region, steps address places in the file; audio clips looped and split on the timeline; the user's own openprocess / openfield are offered in the device list without a scan |
| **snd** (Mark Fell, Mat Steel) | began by copying ("a bit of Mike Ink, a bit of Thomas Brinkmann, a bit of New York house") and "thought we were making house music"; one module per album: a Yamaha FS1R "more or less does all the sounds on *Atavism*", apart from the percussion; no basslines ("we've never done a bassline"), the low end treated as frequency; rhythm as a question of balance ("the right level of drive"); on stage at laptops, with "a definite decision never to nod our heads on stage in time to music"; nothing kept secret ("let them have a go"). [RBMA](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2012/05/mark-fell-interview), [Sound Propositions](https://soundpropositions.com/2015/01/29/sound-propositions-08-mark-fell/) | the same tools as the Mark Fell row; `evolve`; the two-elements-at-a-time discipline is the session grid: scenes that keep two tracks and stop the rest |
| **Gábor Lázár** | software only: his own "compositional interfaces" in Max, "not creating tracks but instruments", the patches "really simple"; "I first create the rhythm and then I play around with the synth and record it in real-time"; early records additive ("lots of oscillators on top of each other"), though "it isn't so important what synthesis I'm using"; "generative techniques", not generative tools, complexity from "overlapping layers"; patterns "rhythmically off-grid"; live, everything generated in real time except "the 10 short drum sounds in my sampler"; with Mark Fell (*The Neurobiology of Moral Decision Making*, The Death of Rave, 2015): two computers connected, nothing agreed beforehand, three hours recorded over a few days in Budapest and cut to 50 minutes, "real-time recordings with a little bit of post-editing". He names no pulse, FM or sync technique in these interviews. [Stray Landings](https://straylandings.co.uk/_content/interviews/2017-03-01-real-time-gabor-lazar-interview/), [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/theres-constant-hype-about-the-instruments-synths-speakers-and-computers-that-producers-use-but-will-your-music-sound-better-because-of-that-gabor-lazar-on-sound-design-generative-techniques-and-designing-his-own-instruments-in-max), [SHAPE](https://shapeplatform.eu/2015/gabor-lazar-im-basically-trying-to-give-different-answers-to-my-own-questions/) | ratchets 1 – 8 with a curve per step (accelerating or slowing bursts); `accelerate`; parameter locks with slides on `st:fm` and `st:tone` (pulse width, frequency); speed x8 and the ms clock |
| **Kindohm** (Mike Hodnick) | TidalCycles live coding: a big `stack` for "two levels of transformation"; euclidean hits (`bd(3,8)`); time shifted on chosen cycles (`every 3 (0.25 <~)`, his `foldEvery [3,4]`); a different change on every 3rd, 4th, 5th, 6th and 7th cycle (shift, `chop`, `speed`, `crush`, `rev`), "that pattern will cycle 420 times before repeating"; polymeter (`{bd ~ ~ bd ~}%4` against `ch*8`); own functions `rip` (a quick backwards rush) and `brakk` (random, time-stretched breaks from pre-sliced loops); his own sounds, not the stock samples; sets improvised from a blank editor, from a prepared skeleton, or composed, depending on the room; algoraves. [TidalCycles blog](https://blog.tidalcycles.org/index.html%3Fp=75.html), [Lullabot](https://www.lullabot.com/podcasts/hacking-culture/mike-hodnick-on-live-coding-with-tidalcycles) | the pattern functions as tools and step settings: euclid, rotate, reverse, conditions 1:2 … 8:8 (every nth pass), chance (degrade), speed, different lengths per track (polymeter), `evolve`; chopped breaks: `bounce` → sampler slices, a slice per step; midi in from a live-coding tool plays the selected track |
| **Peter Blasser** (Ciat-Lonbarde, Shbobo) | paper circuits: printable sheets built with your own parts, component values left open ("randomer is better"); the Shinth, played "by directly touching the circuits", then "safe" touch nodes in a wooden case whose wood "acts as an antenna"; brass "sandrodes", "androgynous nodes that represent both input and output"; the Plumbutter, a "drum and drama" machine on the pulse geometry of the rolz-5: "brown bananas are androgynous pulse nodes", "hot colors are outputs, and cool colors are inputs"; Fourses: "four bounds/bounce oscillators, stacked on top of each other so that their bounds are mutual", balls that "bounce off of each other in the tube, and generate intricate but inter-related chaotic outputs"; playing instability as "stormsurfing"; Shbobo's Shnth and Shtar are programmed in Shlisp, with Fish as its graphical editor, open source since December 2020. No page that could be opened describes the Cocoquantus or Nabra, so nothing is claimed for them. [eContact!](https://econtact.ca/17_4/blasser_ovalsynth.html), [Analogue Haven](https://analoguehaven.com/ciat-lonbarde/plumbutter/), [ModularGrid](https://modulargrid.net/e/ieaskul-f-mobenthey-fourses), [Make](https://makezine.com/article/craft/implied-chaos-sound-circu/), [shbobo-studies](https://github.com/montoyamoraga/shbobo-studies) | the modulator source `bounce` (four ramps at unrelated rates, each turning round where it meets its neighbours: four related, never repeating voltages); a modulator's rate as another modulator's target (cross-modulation); feedback routes between tracks; a control held open from a key, like a touched node |
| **Iannis Xenakis** | *Achorripsis* (1956–57): a matrix of cells whose densities follow Poisson's law; inside a cell the times between events follow an exponential law, the intervals between pitches a linear one and the glissando speeds a gaussian, giving "a table of durations, a table of speeds, and a table of intervals", with "a great liberty of choice" left to the composer; the same scheme run on an IBM 7090 for the ST pieces (ST/10-1, 080262); glissandi as straight lines whose slope is the speed (*Pithoprakta*); sieves: residue classes (modulus, residue) combined by union, intersection and complement, for pitch scales and for rhythm (*Persephassa*); UPIC (CEMAMu, 1977): waveforms and whole pages drawn on a tablet, *Mycenae Alpha* the first piece made entirely on it; players placed among or around the audience (*Terretektorh*, *Persephassa*) and sound rotated from group to group by dynamics. [Formalized Music](https://monoskop.org/images/7/74/Xenakis_Iannis_Formalized_Music_Thought_and_Mathematics_in_Composition.pdf), [sieve theory](https://www.iannis-xenakis.org/en/sieve-theory/), [stochastic music](https://www.iannis-xenakis.org/en/stochastic-music/), [UPIC](https://120years.net/upic-system-iannis-xenakis-france-1977/), [Hofmann](https://cicm.univ-paris8.fr/ColloqueXenakis/papers/Hofmann%28Boris%29.pdf) | pattern tools `cloud` (times between events exponential, intervals with a linear law, gaussian dynamics), `sieve` and `sieve pitch` (residue classes: 3@0 \| 4@1, & intersects, ! excludes); automation lanes drawn freehand (shift-drag) onto `st:tone`'s frequency: arcs become glissandi; slides between locked steps; an encoder's azimuth and elevation as lanes and modulator targets; `st:line` |
| **Bernhard Leitner** | sound as building material since 1968–69: "a line of sound is produced when sound moves along a series of loudspeakers", and "space can be defined by lines of sound"; Soundcube (1969): 64 loudspeakers on each side, 384 individually controllable sources, "the sound is programmed to travel from loudspeaker to loudspeaker", speed, pitch or direction changing; the building elements are "the distribution of loudspeakers, the speed and the direction of the moving sound, the intensity, frequency and the color of sound"; full-scale tests from 1971 (a descending vertical line, inclined lines, loops in grids of speakers) with plain material: percussion rolls, short low synthesizer beats, cello and horn tones at constant pitch; Ton-Tor (1971), Ton-Röhre (1973), Ton-Liege (1975), Ton-Raum TU Berlin (1984, 42 hidden loudspeakers); the whole body hears, "not merely with the ears". [P.U.L.S.E. catalogue](https://monoskop.org/images/3/39/Leitner_Bernhard_PULSE_2008.pdf), [Brooklyn Rail](https://brooklynrail.org/2026/04/architecture/sound-space-and-the-body-bernhard-leitner/), [works list](https://www.bernhardleitner.at/works) | `st:line` on a wide track: each input channel is a line of sound travelling along the track's speakers (pass time on the grid or in seconds, rest at the ends, legato from a hard switch to a melt, there-and-back / round / swinging / at random, a sieve choosing which speakers sound); the user's own Leitner plug-in is offered in the device list without a scan and feeds a 16-channel track |
| **Alex Chechile** | *On the Sensations of Tone* (2010–): pieces that provoke distortion products in the listener's cochlea; two pure tones f1 and f2 (f2 > f1) "within close ratio (typically 1.22 in clinical settings)" give f2−f1 (the quadratic difference tone) and 2f1−f2 (the cubic one), "not present in the acoustic space"; lower ratios can give stronger products, the cubic tone being the more sensitive to ratio; over loudspeakers at roughly 84–95 dB SPL, so kept for concerts; each pair of tones routed to its own pair of speakers, "else intermodulation distortion would result in the speaker"; moving the head makes tones "appear, disappear, and change timbre"; the Ear Tone Toolbox (Max externals, VSTs, OWL patches: DiffTone, DPSpecS, f1half, f1ratio) computes the primaries for a wanted difference tone. [ICMC 2016 paper](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~chechile/eartonetoolbox/Chechile_ICMC16.pdf), [toolbox](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~chechile/eartonetoolbox/main.html), [project notes](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~chechile/220c/220csite.html) | `st:tone`'s second tone: a pair of pure sines a set number of hz or a ratio apart (1.22 by default), one per channel so the difference tone is made in the ear and not in the speaker |
| **La Monte Young** | sustained sine tones as an environment: the Dream House's *The Base 9:7:4 Symmetry in Prime Time* is "35 sine tones stretched across 10 octaves", 20 of them in a narrow band of the seventh octave, the frequencies large prime-numbered ratios (Mersenne primes, twin primes, "Young's Primes") between the 1792nd and 2304th overtones; the tones stand still when the listener does and leap when the head moves; *The Well-Tuned Piano*: just intonation from the factors 2, 3 and 7 only, no 5, every pitch a ratio to E-flat, fifths (3/2) one way and sevenths (7/4) the other. [Gann, Dream House](https://melafoundation.org/gann.htm), [Gann, The Well-Tuned Piano](https://www.kylegann.com/wtp.html) | `st:tone` held by the note's gate as a drone, its `tuning` (equal, 5-limit just, 7-limit after the well-tuned piano, the harmonic series) for key-tracked notes, chords stacked on a step for sine chords, exact hz |
| **William Basinski** | *The Disintegration Loops*: quarter-inch loops from the 1980s, 12 to 16 inches long, slowed fragments of radio; in July 2001, while he copied them from a Revox to a CD burner, the oxide came off the backing on every pass ("I could see dust in the tape path") and the music thinned into gaps; each loop was left to run to its end, the first for about 63 minutes; his part was "to concentrate on what's happening and stay out of the way": a synthesizer countermelody on the first loop only, a Lexicon reverb filling the gaps; six loops in two days. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/classic-tracks-william-basinski-disintegration-loops), [FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2012/09/11/interview-william-basinski) | `st:wear` — a loop of the input that goes round and loses a little on every pass (highs, then dropouts, then level), length on the grid or in seconds; `st:vinyl` and `st:colour` for wow, flutter and hiss; looped audio clips layered on the timeline |

No machine models named in these two pieces. [Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/story-of-a-song-ian-william-craigs-a-simple-hope), [Cokemachineglow](https://cokemachineglow.com/features/interview-ianwilliamcraig-2015/) | as the Basinski row: `st:wear`, with `st:vinyl` and `st:colour` for wow, flutter and hiss |

[A Closer Listen](https://acloserlisten.com/2021/07/27/sound-propositions-020-amulets/), [CDM](https://cdm.link/heres-make-tape-loop-cassette-tape/) | as the Basinski row; here mainly looped audio clips layered on the timeline, and `st:wear` |

### left out, and why
- project-wide odd time signatures: bars stay four beats; odd metres come from pattern lengths (a 20-step pattern against 16), step times and fractional-bar clips.
- microtuning of the suite's drum engines and wb1: they take notes; free tuning is in `st:tone` and in hosted plug-ins.
- a live-coding language (Orca, Fish, TidalCycles themselves): a different instrument; they play the studio over midi, and the standalone sends midi clock.
- measured-ear (HRTF set) binaural decoding: `st:binaural` is a spherical-head model for placing things while arranging; hosted decoders (IEM, SPARTA) do the rest.
- visual artists and inventors the user named for the look of other instruments (not methods for a studio).

## cross-cutting, and where they live in sndstudio
1. non-isochronous and per-step-length sequencing → `step time`, the tools `ratios` and `accelerate`, speed, free clock, ratchets with a curve
2. long-take capture with later excerpting → audio tracks, `capture`, resample project, split / trim
3. dub effects as instruments → throws, sends and mutes played from a computer key (toggle or held) or a midi controller, written as automation with `write` on
4. resampling everywhere → resample track / bus / project to a new audio or sampler track
5. vocal micro-sampling with per-step pitch → `st:sampler` slices; choke groups across tracks, mutes as per-step locks or from a held key
6. precision generators → `st:tone`, `st:data`, `st:noise`
7. tape, varispeed, detune → `st:vinyl`, `st:colour`, drift modulators, pitch locks
8. grid-free audio editing → snap off on the timeline
9. hardware integration → midi clock out, midi in to any track, audio inputs, hosted plug-ins
10. self mastering with side-length awareness → ms0 (auto as a starting point), the bar · minute readout in the header and under the song list
11. sieves and stochastic clouds → the pattern tools `cloud`, `sieve` and `sieve pitch`
12. skipping and worn loops → `st:skip`, `st:wear`
13. lines through speakers → `st:line` on a wide track; an encoder's azimuth and elevation as lanes and modulator targets
14. difference tones and just tuning → `st:tone`'s second tone and its `tuning`
15. evolving patterns → `evolve` and `freeze`; chance per step and per track
